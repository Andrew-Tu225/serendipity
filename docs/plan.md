# Project Concept: A Social Map for Real-World Discovery

**Working tagline:** Discover the world through the experiences of people you trust.

**Concept:** Letterboxd for real-world experiences, built around an interactive map.

## 1. Overview

We're building a social discovery platform that changes how people explore the world around them.

Imagine Google Maps, but instead of relying primarily on ratings and reviews from thousands of strangers, your map is shaped by the experiences of your friends.

Users can explore a familiar interactive map, discover places their friends have visited, read personal reviews, share their own experiences, and bookmark locations they want to visit in the future.

The platform isn't limited to restaurants or cafés. It encompasses virtually anything you can find on a map:

- Restaurants, cafés, and hidden food spots
- Hiking trails, parks, and scenic viewpoints
- Gyms, climbing gyms, and recreational facilities
- Museums, galleries, and local attractions
- Bookstores, shops, and unique businesses
- Interesting locations and experiences that people might otherwise overlook

**The central idea is simple: Turn your friends' real-world experiences into a personalized map of places worth discovering.**

## 2. The Problem

### Maps are useful, but not personal

Google Maps is excellent for finding locations, getting directions, and accessing business information. However, its discovery experience primarily relies on broad public ratings, search relevance, and reviews from strangers.

A place might have thousands of reviews and an excellent rating, but that doesn't necessarily tell you whether you'll personally enjoy it.

Meanwhile, some of the most memorable experiences come from places recommended by friends or discovered through word of mouth.

### Recommendations get lost

Think about how people currently discover places.

A friend posts a restaurant on their Instagram story. You ask where it is, they tell you, and you might make a mental note to visit someday.

Someone mentions an incredible hiking trail during a conversation. You tell yourself you'll remember it.

A friend recommends a small café, climbing gym, or local attraction.

But what happens afterward?

That recommendation gets buried in a direct message, forgotten in a conversation, saved in a random Notes app entry, or lost among dozens of social media posts.

**There's no central place where the locations your friends recommend become discoverable, organized, and actionable.**

### Hidden gems remain hidden

Traditional discovery platforms tend to surface already-popular destinations. Smaller, niche, or lesser-known experiences can be difficult to discover, even when they're exactly what someone would enjoy.

We want to make word-of-mouth discovery easier, more persistent, and more accessible.

## 3. The Solution

A map-first social platform where people share, rate, review, and discover real-world experiences through their social connections.

Instead of viewing locations solely through public ratings, users can explore the world through the perspectives of their friends.

The map becomes a visual collection of people's experiences.

Every review, recommendation, or bookmark contributes to a personalized discovery ecosystem.

### A. A Social Map

The map is the centre of the application.

Users open the app to an interactive map similar to what they're already familiar with, but with an additional social layer.

Locations can be represented by different markers depending on their relationship to the user.

For example:

- Places highly recommended by friends
- Places friends have visited and reviewed
- Places the user has bookmarked
- Potential hidden gems or new discoveries

Selecting a location reveals its information alongside reviews and experiences shared by friends.

Users could explore their local neighbourhood or move the map to a city they're planning to visit and discover recommendations from their social network.

The goal is for the map itself to become a personalized exploration tool.

### B. Experiences, Ratings, and Reviews

Users can log their experiences at places they've visited.

Much like Letterboxd allows people to document their experiences with films, this platform would allow people to document their experiences with physical locations.

Each experience could include:

- A personal rating using the app's own rating system
- A written review about the experience
- Photos taken during the visit
- A recommendation, or reasons someone might want to avoid the location

These reviews are attached to locations on the map, making them accessible to friends exploring that area.

Rather than solely asking, "What does the general public think about this place?", the app helps answer:

**"What did my friends think about this place, and would I enjoy it too?"**

### C. Friends and Social Discovery

The platform revolves around social connections.

Users can add friends, view their profiles, and explore places they've reviewed or recommended.

Over time, a user's profile becomes a collection of their real-world experiences.

For example, someone who's passionate about hiking might have a collection of trails, viewpoints, and outdoor destinations they've visited.

Another friend might be known for discovering interesting restaurants and cafés.

Instead of repeatedly asking these friends for recommendations, users can explore their experiences directly on the map.

Friends help vet places by sharing their genuine opinions, both positive and negative.

This creates an environment where recommendations feel more personal and relevant than anonymous public reviews.

### D. Bookmarking and Saving Places

Bookmarking is one of the core features of the platform.

People constantly come across places they want to visit but don't have a convenient way to keep track of them.

Users should be able to save locations quickly, regardless of whether they've visited them.

For example:

A friend posts an interesting restaurant on their private Instagram story. You want to visit it, but not today.

Instead of leaving the recommendation buried in your messages or writing it down somewhere, you open the app, search for the location, and bookmark it.

You can immediately access its information, see whether your other friends have reviewed it, and return to it later.

Bookmarks should be visible on the map and accessible through a dedicated saved-places section.

**The intention is to turn passing recommendations into places people actually remember and eventually experience.**

### E. Discovering the Unknown

Discovery is the underlying purpose of the entire application.

The unknown doesn't necessarily mean somewhere completely unexplored or undiscovered by the public.

It can mean a place that's unknown to you.

It might be a small family-owned restaurant a few streets away, a beautiful hiking trail outside your city, a unique local activity, or a hidden viewpoint recommended by a friend.

These places already exist. The problem is that people often don't know about them.

By connecting people's experiences to a shared social map, the platform helps surface locations that someone might never have considered otherwise.

Rather than only discovering places through search queries or popularity rankings, users discover them through the people and communities around them.

The app should encourage curiosity, spontaneity, and exploration of new environments.

## 4. Example User Experience

Imagine opening the app while you're in Toronto.

You explore the map and notice several highlighted locations in neighbourhoods around you.

One marker represents a restaurant that multiple friends highly recommended.

Another represents a small bookstore your friend recently visited and reviewed.

Another is a scenic trail that someone in your network discovered.

You tap the restaurant, read your friends' experiences, look through their photos, and decide it looks interesting.

You bookmark it for another day.

Later, you're looking for something to do over the weekend. You open your saved places and see the restaurant alongside several other experiences you've been meaning to try.

Instead of searching endlessly for something interesting to do, you already have a personalized map of potential experiences.

And after visiting one yourself, you can leave your own review, helping your friends discover it too.

**The cycle is: Discover → Save → Experience → Review → Share → Inspire someone else's discovery.**

## 5. What Makes the App Different?

This isn't intended to simply be another Google Maps alternative or a generic social media platform.

The core differences are:

**Friends over strangers:** Personal recommendations and experiences from trusted people are central to the discovery process.

**Experiences over categories:** The platform isn't limited to food, travel, or entertainment. Anything on a map can potentially become an experience worth sharing.

**Map-first social interaction:** Instead of a traditional social feed being the only way to discover content, social activity is directly connected to geographical locations.

**Saving for future exploration:** Bookmarks are treated as a fundamental part of discovery, helping people retain recommendations and turn them into real experiences.

**Discovery over popularity:** The app should help people uncover interesting places they wouldn't ordinarily search for, rather than only reinforcing what's already popular.

## 6. Connection to the Hackathon Theme: Discovering the Unknown

The app enables people to discover the unknown by using the collective experiences of their friends to uncover unfamiliar locations and activities.

Everyone has their own small collection of memorable places. A favourite café, an underrated hiking trail, a unique gym, or somewhere they stumbled upon by accident.

Most of this knowledge stays scattered across conversations, social media, and individual memories.

Our application brings those experiences together geographically, allowing users to explore the world through the perspectives of others.

The more people share their experiences, the more opportunities their friends have to discover something new.

**We aren't creating new destinations. We're helping people discover the experiences already hiding around them.**

## 7. Product Vision

We want to make exploring the real world feel as social and engaging as sharing experiences online.

A platform where people don't just consume recommendations but actively contribute to helping others experience new things.

A place where the map reflects not only what's physically around you, but also the memories, perspectives, and discoveries of the people in your life.

Ultimately, we want someone to open the app and think:

**"I wonder what interesting places my friends have discovered around here."**

## Current implementation

Discover fills the phone above navigation, with floating search, location controls, and place previews.
Google supplies the basemap, global place/city search, and nearby experience destinations through FastAPI.
Unreviewed destinations use smaller neutral custom category pins matching the app. Nearby searches
sample neighborhood areas as users explore; they do not guarantee every Google listing. Existing pins
remain stable as finer samples load on zoom-in. Nearby work runs up to three searches at once and
is limited to 18 attempts per minute per open map, with automatic continuation when slots reopen.
App markers take precedence for matching Google IDs. Provider results remain temporary and create no database rows.
App-reviewed Places come
from PostgreSQL for the visible map area after initial load, panning, zooming, search, or geolocation.
Every reviewed Place is eligible, including low ratings; recommendation ranking remains separate.
Pins remain stable while moving around the map. Buffered app-data coverage is cached in memory,
newly exposed areas load incrementally, and stale ratings refresh quietly after one minute. New
reviews update their marker immediately.
Dense locations cluster rather than being dropped. London, Ontario is the initial camera position; location
permission is requested only on tap.

The app requires sign-in before showing Home or Discover. New and existing incomplete accounts must choose at least one of the 14 place-category interests once before entering. Interests seed recommendation similarity separately from review scores; Auth0 continues to manage identity. Returning accounts go straight home. Public backend read APIs retain public access. Signed-in viewers see the
Experiences of users they follow highlighted as friends. The app uses the existing directed-follow
model and public Experience visibility. Each user's latest visit per Place contributes one rating
(1–5 in half steps). Marker averages are displayed to one decimal.

Signed-in users can review an existing app Place or add a Place by choosing a map point and supplying
a name and category. Review submission persists the Place, Experience, and taste update together,
then refreshes the map. Google IDs optionally link provider searches to app records; provider names,
addresses, and coordinates remain transient search data. Fake discovery pins are removed. No fixtures
are inserted automatically. Friend-management UI, saving/bookmark UI, uploads, and category filters
are still future work. Image storage remains undecided. See [discovery-data.md](discovery-data.md).
The social backend now supports directed follow management, public profiles and display-name search,
Experience likes and flat editable comments, independent private saved reviews/Places, and a
following feed of published Experiences. Saving a new Place can create its app record without a
review. Profile identity remains read-only and Auth0-managed; category interests are persisted separately during first-login onboarding; all Experiences and social read surfaces are
public, while saves and the personalized feed require sign-in. Home now shows real ranked recommendations around London with supporting reviews. Profile uses the authenticated identity, selected interests, and real Experience/saved Place/following counts. Other social frontend integration remains future work.
See [social-api.md](social-api.md) for contracts and the remaining social scope.
Local development can opt into 24 London Places and labeled mock reviews using the repeatable seed
script; a guarded reset script clears local application rows. See [local-demo-data.md](local-demo-data.md).

## 8. Open Questions and Ideas to Explore

_Items marked implemented are settled decisions; the remaining questions can develop as the concept evolves._

**Rating system**

- What should the rating scale look like?
- Should experiences be rated differently depending on their category?
- How much weight should friends' ratings have compared with general ratings?

**Social experience**

- Directed follows are implemented; a friend means someone the viewer follows, with no approval.
- Should profiles resemble a personal experience diary?
- A backend following feed exposes new reviews, ordered by publication time; feed UI is future work.
- Reviews, profiles, likes, comments, and follower/following lists are public. Private accounts and
  friends-only reviews are future work; saved Places and reviews remain private.

**Map and discovery**

- What determines which pins appear on the map?
- How do we distinguish recommended, reviewed, and bookmarked locations?
- How do we surface lesser-known experiences?
- Should users be able to discover recommendations outside their immediate friend network?

**Bookmarking**

- Should users create custom collections or lists?
- Can users share collections with friends?
- How do we make saving places as effortless as possible?

**Long-term experience**

- How do we encourage users to review experiences?
- How do we help users find relevant places when few friends have reviewed their area?
- How do we balance the feeling of social media with the utility of a map?

---

**One-sentence summary:** A map-based social discovery platform where users turn their real-world experiences into reviews, recommendations, and bookmarks that help their friends explore places they might never have discovered otherwise.
